How do I find a reliable sourcing agent in China, especially as a small importer or Amazon FBA seller?

China Partner Hub · Updated 2026-06-29

Comparing suppliers Supplier verification

The situation

"I'm a small importer and Amazon FBA seller. I need help sourcing in China, but I don't know how to tell a reliable sourcing agent from someone who's just adding markup and forwarding messages."

Short answer

Reliable sourcing agents in China for small importers and Amazon FBA sellers are the ones who reduce decision risk before money gets locked in. They are usually not the ones shouting the loudest about low prices or huge supplier networks. They are the ones who can verify suppliers, explain tradeoffs clearly, stay involved after sampling, and create visibility during production.

If you are asking for "who is reliable," the safest first move is not to trust a list of names. It is to judge the process behind the name.

How to think about it

  1. Judge the work after the quote, not just the quote itself. Finding factories is easy. Screening weak suppliers out, checking fit, managing samples, and catching problems before shipment is the real work.
  2. Ask how they make money. If fee logic is vague, "neutral advice" may actually be steering you toward the supplier that pays them better.
  3. Look for operational detail, not sales language. Reliable agents can explain what they check before deposit, how they judge supplier fit, what they do when a sample looks good but scale risk still exists, and what updates you will receive once production starts.
  4. Check whether they stay active after supplier selection. Weak agents are useful only in the sourcing stage. Strong ones stay useful through spec lock, production follow-up, inspection, and shipment readiness.
  5. Use a low-risk test first. A paid verification task, sample management task, or first-order support task tells you more than a discovery call.

What a reliable sourcing agent should be able to answer clearly

  • How do you verify that the supplier is real and relevant for my product before I pay a deposit?
  • How do you tell factory, trader, and mixed-model supplier apart in practice?
  • How do you handle quality control if I am not in China?
  • What exactly will I receive during production: progress updates, photos, inspection reports, issue escalation, or just supplier messages?
  • If something goes wrong, who pushes the supplier, and how early do you usually catch the problem?

If the answers stay vague, you are probably buying confidence instead of control.

Specifics

  • Green flags: transparent fee logic, supplier verification before recommendation, category-fit judgment, concrete QC logic, clear communication on what happens after the sample stage, and a process that still makes sense if the first supplier is rejected.
  • Red flags: all the emphasis is on price and MOQ, no clear explanation of how they are paid, no method for distinguishing factory from trader, and no explanation of what happens after the order is placed.
  • For small importers and Amazon FBA sellers, reliability matters more than size. A smaller team that actually checks details is often safer than a bigger one that mainly sells confidence.

A practical way to choose

  1. Shortlist two or three candidates.
  2. Give each one the same real product brief.
  3. Ask each one to explain supplier logic, not just quote range.
  4. Pay for a small verification or sample-management task before trusting anyone with a full order.

That process is more reliable than choosing based on branding, LinkedIn polish, or who replies fastest.
