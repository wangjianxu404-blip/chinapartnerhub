How do I verify a Chinese supplier is a real factory before paying a deposit?

China Partner Hub · Updated 2026-06-29

Supplier verification Payment & terms

The situation

"I'm about to pay a deposit to a Chinese supplier. Before I wire anything, how do I verify they're a real factory and not just a trader or a polished online profile?"

Short answer

Before paying a deposit, verify four things together: the legal company identity, whether the product fits their real manufacturing specialty, whether there is real current operating evidence behind the claim, and whether their production answers hold up under detail. A business license alone is not enough. A sample alone is not enough. An Alibaba badge alone is definitely not enough.

How to think about it

  1. Separate company existence from factory reality. A supplier can be a real registered business and still not be the actual manufacturer behind your order. Legal existence is only the first layer.
  2. Verify the identity and payment chain first. Get the full company name in Chinese and English, review the business license, and confirm the bank account matches the company you are speaking with. A mismatch is a stop sign.
  3. Check whether the product is actually inside their strength zone. A real factory can still be the wrong factory. Ask whether they regularly make similar products, in similar materials, at similar quality and order size.
  4. Look for physical operating evidence. Recent workshop video, live walk-throughs, process-specific machinery, and in-process goods tell you more than polished factory marketing photos.
  5. Ask operational questions that are hard to fake. Which parts are made in-house, what the common defect points are, how lead time breaks down by stage, and what is outsourced. Genuine makers usually answer with concrete production language.

A pre-deposit verification checklist

  • Confirm the exact legal company name.
  • Check whether the bank account name matches that company.
  • Check whether the product is truly inside their normal manufacturing range.
  • Ask what is made in-house and what is outsourced.
  • Ask for recent production evidence for similar products, not generic factory media.
  • Ask category-specific technical questions that a real maker should answer naturally.
  • If the order matters, use live local verification before wiring money.

Specifics

  • Strong signs: company and bank details align, product-specific production answers are natural, current production evidence matches the category, the supplier can explain what is in-house versus outsourced.
  • Warning signs: changing company names, a bank account that does not match, resistance to showing current production, quoting unrelated categories equally well, much stronger sales language than production language.
  • A good sample helps, but it proves only that one acceptable piece can be made. It does not prove repeatability at scale.

When to slow down immediately

  • The payee name changes late in the process.
  • The supplier says the factory and exporter are different, but cannot explain the structure clearly.
  • They are happy to discuss price but weak on process.
  • They can show polished photos but avoid current shop-floor evidence.
  • They quote many unrelated product categories with equal confidence.
