From link to workable brief

The buyer knew the installation. The supplier listings did not yet match it.

A marketplace link can identify a product family without proving that the listed size, material, voltage, protection level, or accessories fit the actual job.

01

Check the link the buyer already found

The original 1688 listing did not show the required 40 × 20 mm channel. We reviewed it instead of treating the link as a finished product choice.

02

Find another option and give the buyer the link

We found another listing that appeared closer to the requested dimensions and sent it directly to the buyer. The buyer could inspect the page and replied that it looked suitable.

03

Confirm how the product would actually be installed

We asked about exterior dimensions, empty channel versus a complete light, LED width, outdoor exposure, material, colour, bending direction, continuous lengths, fixing clips, end caps, and connectors.

04

Search again when the first options did not meet the brief

Listings were compared across voltage, waterproofing, width, height, and LED density. Several options met only part of the requirement. The buyer also sent another reference link, and we continued the search from there.

Requirement record

The specification was built around the façade, not around a marketplace title.

The total length was later clarified as 67 metres across six sections. The longest section was 12 metres, which also raised a voltage-drop question for a 24 V strip powered from one end.

Supplier communication

When the listing and the seller did not agree, the uncertainty stayed visible.

Contradictory waterproof information

One product page indicated that the item was not waterproof, while the seller described it as suitable for outdoor rain. We told the buyer that the actual protection level remained unclear instead of turning the seller's reply into a confirmed fact.

Not every high-density strip fit

A 280-LED-per-metre COB option looked stronger on density, but the buyer rejected it because the strip shape was too narrow and too tall for the intended installation.

No adhesive backing

The available red three-row channel did not include rear adhesive. For a long installation, we explained that mechanical fixing could be more durable than relying on adhesive alone.

Accessories and sealing

The accessory list was confirmed with end caps, omega clips, and power connections. The supplier was also asked to seal both ends for outdoor use.

Logistics comparison

We kept looking after the first workable freight quote.

The first estimate used an available air route. A silicone-channel supplier later offered a special route to the Canary Islands at a much lower rate. The cheaper route was slower and had a less predictable departure, so both the saving and the limitation were shown.
